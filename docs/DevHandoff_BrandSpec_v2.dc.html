Part 1 · Type

One voice for numbers, one for work

Crimson Pro carries the identity — titles, wordmark, player name, hero numbers, and the tile numerals, set in its title weight with lining + tabular figures forced so every digit is the same width and sits on the baseline (its default old-style figures would drop below it). Overpass does everything functional at Regular (400) — a step thinner than before — with 700 kept only for button labels.

Part 2 · Skins (tiles only)

Three palettes, twelve colours

A skin is purely a tile palette — a new axis, independent of light/dark and of player identity. Each carries the full 12 tile colours (rack sizes go to 12) in the {fill,edge,ink} shape the build already uses. Skins no longer set an accent: chrome colour comes from the player (Part 3).

Warm, earthy, handmade. Distinctness is traded a little for cohesion (that's what Colorful is for); one cool slate anchor at 7 keeps the mid-set readable.

Colorful · 12
The CVD-safe set, kept

The current palette — 8 colour-blind-safe + 4 graceful (9–12). Unchanged. The accessible choice, offered as a first-class skin.

Monochrome · 12
The player's hue, stepped

All open tiles are the active player's colour; each number takes one tonal step on a 12-stop lightness ramp so 1–12 stay told apart. Numeral + step do the work. Derived at render, no static hexes.

Selection highlight
Opposite of the theme

The picked-tile outline is var(--fg) — white on dark, near-black on light — for maximum contrast in any skin. Not the player colour, not the skin.

Independent axes:
Skin: Brand / Colorful / Mono × Theme: Light / Dark (+ overpay XOR) × Player colour → chrome
Skin + theme live in the App tab and persist in storage.js; player colour is per active turn.
Part 3 · The player colours the UI

Whose turn it is, at a glance

The active player's identity colour (the CVD-safe swatch they pick at setup) tints the chrome: name, timer, settings cog, boost counters, the boost toast, the promoted button, and the message. Player colours are theme-independent constants — a player is "the teal one" in either theme; only the surrounding neutrals flip.

On dark
The fill, straight

Text and icons use the player's fill directly (e.g. teal #26A69A); the promoted button is a solid fill with a cream ink.

On light
The edge, for text

Some fills fail 4.5:1 on the pale ground, so text/icons drop to the hue's dark partner (edge); fills still back the buttons/toast with cream ink.

One promoted at a time
Never two lit buttons

Exactly one primary carries the player colour; any sibling primary drops to a muted neutral (Roll dims once Confirm is the move).

Tile state is unchanged: open = full-chroma + raised + solid numeral; closed = desaturated #5C625F + pressed inset + dim hollow numeral — three redundant non-hue cues, so state survives greyscale in every skin, mono included. The avatar (app/avatars/NN.png, head/bust crop in a player-colour ring) replaces the potato on the Play + turn-card screens.

Part 4 · The anti-jump rule

Every zone reserves its space

The live build already fixes most zone heights. The redesign holds the line and merges the old settings strip into one row: Settings cog · two permanent boost chips · a name-only toast, all under the identity row. The toast is right-aligned and, after a beat, shrinks into its counter and increments it; boost copy stays in the bottom safe area. The rack is the one flexible zone.

1 · Identity avatar+name / timer · topmost
2 · Chrome + boost cog · 2 chips · toast
3 · Rack flexes · absorbs slack
4 · Selection sum 1 line · fixed
5 · Dice · buttons · safe area all fixed

  • One promoted button; the other gets .is-muted

  • Boost inventory → two permanent icon+counter chips

  • Open/closed 3-cue tile-state model
  • Reserved-height Play zones
  • Rules engine, motion thresholds, boost award model
